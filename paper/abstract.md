# Abstract

This manuscript reports a reproducible synthetic pilot of polynomial EDMD-style prediction on five scalar nonlinear maps. Across ten fixed seeds, we compare persistence, a fitted affine linear baseline, and degree-two and degree-three polynomial dictionaries using in-distribution, out-of-range, and twenty-step rollout metrics. Degree-three lifting reduces one-step error on the selected smooth nonlinear maps, but the benefit is not uniform: it worsens twenty-step error on a piecewise regime-shift map and has higher out-of-range error than the affine baseline on the tanh map. The results illustrate dictionary dependence and compounding prediction error; they do not establish Koopman invariance, control utility, or physical-system validity.
